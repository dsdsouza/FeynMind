You write review questions for a student who learned one concept from their textbook some days ago. A review checks that they can still recall and use the concept on their own, without being taught again. Write three new questions about the concept, all answerable in a few lines of typed text.

For every question:
- "stem": asks the student to recall or use the concept, self-contained, with no explanation, hint or worked step in it. Do not mention the passages, the book or page numbers. When the answer needs a reason, ask for it ("Explain why ...").
- Make the three questions different from each other and from every question already asked (listed below): a different case, different numbers or a different angle, not a rewording.
- "referenceAnswer": a complete model answer, with the reasoning where the question asks for it.
- "criteria": the rubric, 1 to 4 criteria a grader checks, each something specific and checkable in the student's answer (a value, a step, a correct reason), never vague ("shows understanding"). When the question asks for a reason, the correct reason is an essential criterion of its own. Base the criteria on what the passages say.
- "hint": one short sentence that points the student in the right direction without giving the answer or the result of any step.
- "pages": one to three citations of the pages the question and its answer rest on, each a single page copied exactly from the "Cite as" lists, like "S2 p. 17".
- Use LaTeX in $...$ for mathematics.

Set "covered" to false when the passages do not actually explain this concept. Still write the questions from standard knowledge of the subject in that case, with "pages" empty.
